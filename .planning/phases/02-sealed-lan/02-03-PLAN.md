<objective>
Sealed LAN. Today LanLink sends the 8-byte static PeerID as its first frame and every mesh
packet in clear, and the UDP beacon is the text `OFFM1 <peerIdHex> <port>`. A packet capture
therefore shows who is on the network and every packet header. After this plan, everything a
LAN socket or beacon sends is sealed with the unit seal key, and the only identifier inside
the seal on discovery surfaces is the hourly rotating ID.
</objective>

<tasks>
<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 1: LanManager unit configuration</name>
  <description>
    `LanManager.configure(UnitKeys keys, List<List<int>> rosterPublicKeys)` builds one
    `SealedEnvelope(keys.sealKey, clock)` and `RosterResolver.create(keys.idKey, roster,
    clock)`. An optional constructor `clock` (default `DateTime.now`) drives both the seal
    freshness check and the epoch. `start()` and `connect()` throw `StateError` while
    unconfigured. `ChatController._apply` derives `UnitKeys` from the roster's unit secret and
    calls `configure` on every roster change. A link keeps the configuration it was opened
    with.
  </description>
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 2: Sealed LanLink with rotating-ID hello</name>
  <description>
    `LanLink.attach(socket, envelope:, resolver:, localRotatingId:, isDialer:, idleTimeout:,
    onDrop:)`. All writes go through one future chain, so frames leave in order although
    sealing is async. All reads go through a second chain. Handshake: each side first sends
    a sealed random 16-byte challenge. When the peer's challenge arrives, the side sends its
    hello: rotating ID for the current epoch (`rotatingId(idKey, ownPublicKey, epoch)`,
    computed per link) || the peer's challenge || its role byte. The receiver opens the
    hello, checks its length, its own challenge and the opposite role, resolves the ID to a
    roster member and completes `remotePeerId` with that member's static PeerID. Any failure
    closes the link at once. After the handshake, a frame that fails to open increments a
    drop counter and is discarded, and an empty frame is a keepalive. A periodic tick every
    `idleTimeout / 3` sends a keepalive and closes the link after three ticks with no opened
    frame. State is checked after every await so a close during a pending open or seal is
    safe. LanManager closes a link that does not finish its handshake within 5 seconds,
    resolves duplicate PeerIDs as in must_haves, and bumps a generation counter in stop() so
    a handshake, dial or beacon that finishes afterwards is closed instead of registered.
  </description>
</task>

<task type="auto">
  <name>Task 3: Sealed beacon</name>
  <description>
    The beacon is `seal(rotatingId(8) || port(2 BE))`, recomputed each send. The receiver
    opens it, requires exactly 10 bytes, resolves the ID, ignores itself (resolves to its own
    PeerID), non-members, already linked peers, repeats of sealed bytes already seen, peers
    with a dial in flight or backing off, and anything past 8 concurrent dials, then dials.
    Beacons that fail to open are
    counted with the link drops (`LanManager.droppedFrames`). The beacon handler catches every
    error, so a hostile datagram cannot fail the zone.
  </description>
</task>

<known_limits>
- Roster v1 is unsigned: any holder of the unit secret can claim any member's rotating ID in
  a hello, and so take that member's link slot until the real member links again (newer
  wins in the same direction) or the squatter goes silent past the idle timeout. Message
  content stays protected by the pairwise Noise sessions.
- Transparent relay: a party without the key who gets a member to dial it (a beacon that
  member did not already see, replayed from the attacker's address, or a typed address) can
  dial a second member and pass both handshakes through. Challenges and roles then match,
  so the two members link through the relay. It sees only sealed frames but can drop or
  delay them, and can forward keepalives alone to keep the link up.
- Beacon replay: a member ignores sealed beacon bytes it has already seen, but a beacon it
  missed can be replayed (from any source address) within the 10-minute freshness window.
  Each such dial that fails puts that member's beacon dials into a 3 s backoff, so a steady
  stream of replays can delay rediscovery of that member.
- Sealed data frames have no per-link replay protection; a replayed mesh packet is caught by
  the mesh seen cache.
- No epoch key chain: a captured unit secret opens all recorded LAN traffic headers.
- Frame lengths (2-byte prefix) and timing stay visible on the wire; the seal hides content,
  not traffic shape.
</known_limits>
